**TL;DR:** Klaviyo doesn't want peptide or GLP-1 brands on its platform, and its trust & safety team proves it every quarter with a fresh wave of account suspensions. The best alternative is **self-hosted FluentCRM** — you own the list, you own the sending, there's nothing for anyone to shut off. If you need a hosted platform, **Sendlane** is the one that actually tolerates high-risk categories. **Omnisend** works in narrow, adjacent cases. Mailchimp and "Mailgun as your marketing platform" are traps — skip both.

## Why does Klaviyo keep banning peptide brands?

Klaviyo is built for mainstream ecommerce — apparel, beauty, home goods. Its acceptable use policy quietly excludes anything that touches supplements sold with health-adjacent language, and peptides sit right in that blast radius. Semaglutide, tirzepatide, research compounds — the category triggers automated review the moment volume picks up.

Here's the part that actually hurts: it's rarely your fault. A single flagged campaign, a compliance officer having a slow day, an algorithm pattern-matching your subject lines against banned-substance keywords — any of it can get your **Klaviyo account suspended with a list you can't export**. You don't get a warning. You get a locked dashboard and a support ticket that goes nowhere, while your best customers sit on a list you built for years and can no longer reach.

That's the real reason brands go looking for alternatives. Not pricing. Not features. Survival.

## What's the best Klaviyo alternative for a peptide brand?

**Self-hosted FluentCRM**, full stop. It runs on WordPress, which means it runs on infrastructure you control. There's no platform trust & safety team reviewing your campaigns, because there's no platform — just your server, your database, your list. Nobody can suspend an account that doesn't exist.

This matters more in this category than almost any other, because the risk isn't hypothetical. We've watched brands lose six-figure lists overnight when a hosted provider decided peptides were more liability than they wanted. FluentCRM removes that liability entirely. You still need SPF, DKIM, and DMARC configured correctly on a dedicated sending subdomain, and you still need someone managing deliverability — self-hosted doesn't mean set-and-forget. But the account can't be pulled out from under you. We've written the full breakdown on [self-hosted email on FluentCRM](/blog/self-hosted-email-peptide-brands-fluentcrm) if you want the technical detail on setup and warm-up.

The tradeoff: FluentCRM asks more of you operationally than a plug-and-play SaaS tool. That's exactly why brands hire us to run it.

## Is Sendlane actually better than Klaviyo for this category?

Yes, for brands that want a hosted platform without the self-hosted lift. Sendlane is our default recommendation when a client isn't ready to own infrastructure. It has automation, segmentation, and flows on par with Klaviyo — but critically, it tolerates high-risk categories instead of treating them as a liability to purge. That distinction is the entire ballgame.

Sendlane isn't immune to review, and it's not a replacement for having your compliance house in order. But it's built with an understanding that "high-risk" doesn't mean "not allowed," which is more than Klaviyo or Mailchimp can say. If you want automated flows, deliverability support, and a platform that won't panic when it sees the word "tirzepatide" in a subject line, this is where we put clients second, right behind self-hosted.

## When does Omnisend make sense?

Rarely, and only in specific situations. Omnisend can work for brands adjacent to peptides — general supplement DTC, wellness products without the direct GLP-1 or research-peptide framing. It's not our default for a straight peptide or GLP-1 brand, and we don't position it as one. If you're weighing it against Klaviyo specifically, we go deeper on the tradeoffs in [Klaviyo vs Omnisend for peptide ecommerce](/blog/klaviyo-vs-omnisend-peptide-ecommerce) — worth reading before you commit, because the fit is narrower than Omnisend's marketing suggests.

## What about Mailchimp or Mailgun?

Skip both. Mailchimp's policies on supplement and health-adjacent marketing are, if anything, tighter than Klaviyo's — you're trading one ban risk for a nearly identical one, with a worse automation suite attached. We've seen brands migrate off Klaviyo onto Mailchimp thinking they'd found safety, only to get flagged within a quarter.

Mailgun is a different problem entirely. It's a **transactional email API** — built for password resets and order confirmations, not marketing campaigns. Brands sometimes reach for it because it's cheap and technically capable of sending bulk email, but it has no real marketing automation, no list management built for campaigns, and using a transactional API as your marketing backbone tends to tank deliverability fast. SendGrid falls in the same bucket. Neither belongs in this conversation as a Klaviyo alternative — they're solving a different problem.

If you're currently on Klaviyo and running real volume, don't wait for the suspension email to plan your exit. Migrate before you're forced to, on your timeline, with your list intact. The brands that get burned are almost always the ones who assumed they'd have warning.
