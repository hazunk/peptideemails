**TL;DR:** We ranked six email platforms specifically for peptide and high-risk supplement senders, not for the average ecommerce brand doing candles and t-shirts. Our verdict: **self-hosted FluentCRM first**, **Sendlane second**, **Omnisend a distant, situational third**. Klaviyo and Mailchimp — the two platforms every generalist agency will tell you to use — are exactly the wrong pick for this category, and we'll show you the math on why.

We only work with peptide and GLP-1 brands. We don't do skincare, we don't do apparel, we don't do "wellness" in the vague sense — we do research peptides, semaglutide and tirzepatide compounding-adjacent brands, and the supplement companies that live next door to them. That means our platform opinions aren't theoretical. They come from watching real brands get suspended, frozen, and shut down, and from rebuilding their infrastructure so it doesn't happen again.

## What actually determines "best" for a peptide brand?

For a normal DTC brand, "best email platform" means flows, segmentation, and price per contact. For a peptide brand, those criteria are still real, but they're criteria two and three. Criterion one is **survival**: will this platform still be sending your emails in six months, or will a compliance bot flag your account and lock you out of your own list with zero warning?

That's not a hypothetical. Peptide and GLP-1 brands get flagged constantly — not always because of a real violation, but because automated trust-and-safety systems pattern-match on keywords like "semaglutide" or "peptide" and shut first, ask questions later. So the ranking below weighs **ownership and ban risk** as heavily as automation and deliverability. A platform with a gorgeous flow-builder is worthless if it suspends your account the week of a launch.

## Why FluentCRM wins for peptide and GLP-1 brands

**FluentCRM is self-hosted.** That single fact is why it sits at number one. It runs on your WordPress install, through your own SMTP relay, on your own sending subdomain, with your own SPF, DKIM, and DMARC records. There is no trust-and-safety team at FluentCRM reading your subject lines. There is no "your account has been suspended" email that shows up the morning of a launch. You own the list, you own the sends, and nobody can take it away because nobody is holding it hostage in the first place.

We've written the full breakdown of this setup in [self-hosted email on FluentCRM](/blog/self-hosted-email-peptide-brands-fluentcrm), because the "how" matters as much as the "why." Short version: you get real automation — tagging, segmentation, sequences, abandoned cart — without renting your customer list from a platform that can revoke access on a policy update you never read.

The tradeoff is real too. Self-hosted means you're responsible for your own deliverability reputation, your own uptime, your own updates. That's a lot to hand to a brand doing $8k a month. It's a very different equation for a brand doing $200k a month with a real merchant account and a real compliance process. That's the point where we start most of our clients on FluentCRM from day one — we've taken brands in this category from startup to 7 and 8 figures, and infrastructure ownership is one of the first decisions we make with them, not a fix we bolt on after a suspension.

## Where Sendlane fits — and when it's the right call

Sendlane is our second pick because it's the rare hosted platform that actually tolerates high-risk categories instead of treating "peptide" as a banned keyword. It's built for ecommerce, the automation is strong, and their deliverability team knows how to work a sending domain. If a brand isn't ready to manage self-hosted infrastructure — no internal ops person, no bandwidth to own a WordPress-based sending stack — Sendlane is where we put them.

It's still a hosted platform, which means the list technically lives on someone else's servers. That's the risk we're always managing down, even with a provider that's comfortable with this category. It's a real second-place finish, not a consolation prize.

## Where Omnisend fits — rarely, and only for adjacent brands

Omnisend is a distant third, and "distant" is doing real work in that sentence. We only reach for it with supplement DTC brands sitting adjacent to peptides — general wellness, not compound-specific research peptides — where enforcement tends to be looser. Even then it's occasional, not standard. We don't default clients to Omnisend. If you're running semaglutide or tirzepatide-adjacent messaging, Omnisend isn't built with your risk profile in mind, and we won't pretend otherwise.

## Why Klaviyo and Mailchimp are the wrong pick here

This is the part every generalist agency skips, because most of them have never watched it happen. Klaviyo and Mailchimp are excellent platforms — for brands selling shoes, coffee, and supplements with no restricted ingredients. For peptide brands, they're close to the worst possible choice, for one simple reason: **their trust-and-safety systems actively police this category**, and they don't give warnings.

We've had a Klaviyo account get suspended with a six-figure list attached — a list the brand could not export, could not message, could not recover in time for the launch it was built for. That's not a scare story, it's a Tuesday in this industry. Mailchimp behaves the same way, often faster, flagging accounts on keyword triggers before a single campaign even sends. A generalist agency will put a peptide brand on Klaviyo because it's the platform they know. That's exactly how peptide brands get banned — the agency isn't wrong about Klaviyo being good, they're wrong about it being good for you. We go deeper on this comparison in [best Klaviyo alternatives for high-risk brands](/blog/best-klaviyo-alternatives-high-risk-peptide-brands).

Mailgun deserves a mention only to rule it out. It's a transactional API — receipts, password resets, shipping notifications. It was never built for marketing campaigns, segmentation, or flows, and pairing it with a peptide brand's newsletter program is a mismatch of tool to job, not a strategy.

## The verdict, plainly

FluentCRM first, because ownership beats convenience when a suspension can end your business overnight. Sendlane second, because it's the strongest hosted option that actually accepts this category. Omnisend a distant third, reserved for adjacent supplement brands, never the default. Klaviyo and Mailchimp are not on this list as viable options — they're on this list as the mistake we get called in to fix.

## A quick note on compliance, regardless of platform

No platform choice fixes bad copy. Every send needs to stay research-only, structure-function framing under "intended use" per 21 CFR 201.128 — no disease claims, no dosage guidance, no consumption testimonials. A well-chosen platform keeps your list alive. Compliant copy keeps the FDA off your back. You need both, and most agencies only know how to give you one.

This is what [email marketing for peptide brands](/blog/email-marketing-for-peptide-brands) actually requires: the right infrastructure, matched with copy that won't draw a warning letter. It's not a generalist skill set — it's the only thing we do. If you want your list built on a platform that can't be pulled out from under you, that's the conversation to have with us.
