**TL;DR:** For general ecommerce, Omnisend is the more ecommerce-native, lower-risk pick, and Klaviyo's segmentation runs deeper — a real tradeoff, not a coin flip. For peptide and GLP-1 brands specifically, that debate is beside the point: both platforms carry real suspension risk for this category. We default to self-hosted FluentCRM first, Sendlane second, and treat Omnisend as an occasional fit for adjacent supplement brands only — never our first call for a pure peptide list.

## Klaviyo vs Omnisend for general ecommerce — the honest version

Strip away the category risk for a second and judge these two on the merits, because the merits are real.

**Klaviyo's advantage is segmentation depth.** Its flow builder and predictive analytics let you slice a list by purchase behavior, predicted lifetime value, browse activity, and custom properties in ways Omnisend doesn't match. If you're running a mature ecommerce program with complex customer journeys, Klaviyo's segmentation is genuinely ahead.

**Omnisend's advantage is that it was built ecommerce-first, not email-first.** SMS and email live in the same automation builder from day one. Its pricing tends to bundle SMS credits more generously at the entry tier, and its onboarding assumes you're a Shopify or WooCommerce store — because that's who it was designed for. For a brand that wants channel unification without stitching two platforms together, Omnisend gets you there faster.

Klaviyo also tends to charge more as your list grows, and its overage pricing surprises people who don't watch subscriber counts closely. Omnisend's pricing posture is friendlier at small-to-mid list sizes, though it trades some of that automation sophistication to get there.

If you sold home goods, apparel, or mainstream supplements, we'd tell you to pick based on those two factors — segmentation depth versus ecommerce-native simplicity — and move on with your day.

## Why neither is what we run for a peptide brand

Here's the part the generic comparison articles won't tell you, because they're not written for this category.

Klaviyo's acceptable use policy restricts peptide-adjacent content outright. It's not a gray area you might slip past — it's a documented category the platform treats as high-risk, and enforcement catches up with brands once volume or a complaint triggers review. A Klaviyo or Mailchimp account suspended with a list you can't export is the single most common horror story we hear from peptide founders who come to us after the fact. They don't lose a subscription. They lose the list, the flow history, and the domain reputation they spent a year building.

Omnisend is a step better in practice — it doesn't carry the same explicit restricted-category language Klaviyo does, and we've seen it work for adjacent supplement DTC brands with a lighter risk profile. But "a step better" is not the same as "built for this." It's not a platform designed to tolerate peptide-specific sending, and treating it as a safe default is a bet, not a strategy. If you want the fuller list of what to avoid and why, we wrote up the [best Klaviyo alternatives for high-risk brands](/blog/best-klaviyo-alternatives-high-risk-peptide-brands) — Omnisend included, with the caveats attached.

The risk isn't limited to the email platform, either. Payment processors freeze merchant accounts overnight for this category more often than most founders expect going in. Stack platform suspension risk on top of processor risk, and the brands that survive are the ones who treated infrastructure as a decision, not an afterthought.

## What we actually run: FluentCRM first, Sendlane second

Our default recommendation, in order:

1. **Self-hosted FluentCRM.** You own the database. You own the server. There's no acceptable use policy to violate because there's no platform to violate it against. This is the only setup where "suspended overnight" simply isn't a failure mode.
2. **Sendlane.** When self-hosting isn't feasible yet — new team, no dev resources, need something running this week — Sendlane is our hosted fallback. It's built to tolerate high-risk ecommerce categories that Klaviyo and Mailchimp explicitly restrict.
3. **Omnisend, occasionally.** Only for brands closer to general supplement DTC than pure peptide research chemicals, and only with eyes open about where the tolerance ends.

Notice what's not on that list at any position: Klaviyo, Mailchimp, or raw transactional tools like Mailgun trying to double as a marketing platform. None of them were built for what you're selling.

## Where does compliance fit into the platform decision?

Platform choice solves suspension risk. It doesn't solve the other way brands get shut down — an FDA warning letter triggered by one claim in one email. No platform protects you from your own copy. Disease claims, dosage instructions, and consumption testimonials are the three fastest ways to turn a marketing email into a regulatory problem, regardless of whether you sent it from Klaviyo, Omnisend, or FluentCRM. Research-only, structure-function language under 21 CFR 201.128 is the line, and it has to hold in every send, not just the ones someone remembers to review.
