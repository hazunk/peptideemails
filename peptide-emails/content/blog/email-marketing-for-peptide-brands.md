## Why is email the only channel a peptide brand actually controls?

Meta will ban your ad account for mentioning semaglutide in a caption. TikTok will do it faster. Google Ads treats "research peptides" like a live grenade. None of that is a rumor — it's the baseline operating reality of this category, and if you've launched a brand you already know it.

Your **email list** is different. It doesn't live inside a platform that can decide overnight your product category isn't welcome anymore. It lives in a database you control, tied to customers who already bought from you, reachable the moment you hit send. Instagram can deplatform you. Your list can't deplatform itself.

That's why every serious peptide brand we've worked with — from the ones doing six figures a year to the ones doing eight — treats email as the primary revenue channel, not the afterthought bolted on after the Shopify launch. Paid social gets you the first sale, maybe. Email gets you the second, third, and fifteenth.

## Which five flows actually drive revenue?

Campaigns get attention. Flows get revenue — automated, running in the background, converting while you sleep. Five matter most in this category.

**Welcome.** The first three to five emails after opt-in. This is where you build trust with **Certificate of Analysis (COA)** transparency, explain your sourcing, and set research-only framing before anyone reaches for a product page.

**Abandoned cart.** Peptide buyers research hard before purchasing — high price points, high scrutiny. A well-timed cart recovery sequence, sent within hours, recovers purchases that would otherwise evaporate.

**Replenishment / reorder.** This is the flow generalist agencies miss because they don't understand the category's rhythm. Peptide and GLP-1 protocols run on **4–8 week cycles**, not the 90-day reorder windows apparel brands plan around. Time this flow to the actual usage cycle and it becomes your highest-ROI automation, full stop.

**Post-purchase.** Storage guidance, reconstitution logistics (handled carefully), and COA reinforcement. This flow does double duty: it reduces support tickets and builds the trust that drives the next order.

**Winback.** Lapsed customers who've gone quiet. A disciplined winback sequence, segmented by how long they've been dark, recovers revenue most brands write off as gone.

## How do you keep a high-risk sender out of spam?

Peptide brands are high-risk senders by default — new domains, high-scrutiny content, aggressive send volume during launches. That combination is exactly what triggers spam filtering if you don't set the foundation correctly.

Start with the authentication trio: **SPF, DKIM, and DMARC**, all properly configured, all aligned. Send from a dedicated **sending subdomain**, never your root domain — it isolates your sender reputation so a rocky launch week doesn't tank deliverability for your transactional email too. Run a proper **domain warm-up**: ramp volume gradually over several weeks rather than blasting your full list on day one, which is the fastest way to get throttled by Gmail and Yahoo.

Speaking of which — **Google and Yahoo's bulk sender rules** aren't optional guidance anymore. One-click unsubscribe, spam complaint rates under 0.3%, authenticated sending. Miss any of it and your emails route straight to spam regardless of how good the copy is. **List hygiene** — regularly removing unengaged and bounced addresses — isn't busywork. It's the difference between a list that reaches the inbox and one that doesn't.

## What can you actually say in an email?

This is where brands get an FDA warning letter, not from selling the product but from how they talk about it. The governing concept is **21 CFR 201.128** — "intended use." The FDA doesn't just look at your label. It looks at your marketing language as evidence of what you're claiming the product does. Say "helps with weight loss" or "improves diabetes management" in an email, and you've just handed a regulator the exact evidence they need.

The fix isn't complicated, it's disciplined: **no disease claims, no dosage or administration guidance, no consumption testimonials.** Frame everything as research-only, structure-function language. Lead with **COA** transparency instead of outcome promises — it builds more trust anyway, because sophisticated buyers in this category already know to ask for one.
