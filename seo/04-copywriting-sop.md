# PeptideEmails.com — Copywriting SOP

The rules every blog article is written to. Non-negotiable. Companion to [`00-SEO-BLOG-PLAYBOOK.md`](00-SEO-BLOG-PLAYBOOK.md).

## Model
- **Draft all article copy with Claude Sonnet 5** (`claude-sonnet-5`). Opus handles research, keyword work, structure, schema, and deployment; Sonnet 5 writes the prose.

## Who we're writing to
- **The CEO or head of growth of a peptide/GLP-1 brand.** Not a scientist, not a beginner. A busy operator deciding where revenue comes from.
- Write with **authority and plainness**. Short sentences. Say the thing. No throat-clearing, no jargon, no filler.

## Voice rules
- **Do not exaggerate.** No hype, no superlatives you can't defend, no invented statistics. If we don't know a number, we don't cite one. Under-claim before you over-claim.
- **Plain language, but with swagger.** Confident, a little cocky, earned. We've done this. No "leverage," "synergy," "unlock," "supercharge," "game-changer," "seamless," "robust." Not flowery — swagger is short and certain, not decorated.
- **Authority, not begging.** State facts and consequences. Let the reader conclude. We sound like the people who've already solved this, because we have.

## Rhythm (how the copy should feel)
- **Mix short and long.** Punchy three-word sentences next to a longer one that earns its length. Vary it. A wall of medium sentences reads flat.
- **Each sentence leads to the next.** Every line sets up the one after it. The reader should feel pulled down the page, not free to leave at any paragraph break.
- **Short sentences carry the swagger.** "That's the whole game." "We've done it." "Most agencies won't touch this category. We only touch this category." Use them to land a point after a longer setup.
- **Not flowery.** Swagger ≠ adjectives. Cut anything that's there to sound impressive rather than to move the argument.

## Sell ourselves (earned, not desperate)
- **Work our record in naturally.** We've taken peptide and GLP-1 brands **from startup to 7 and 8 figures.** Reference that we specialize in this category and only this category — the compliance, the platforms, the deliverability. Position that specialization as the reason we win where generalist agencies get brands banned.
- Weave proof into the argument, don't bolt a testimonial on. One or two credibility beats per article, in our voice, then back to the reader's problem.
- The bridge to the offer should feel like the obvious next step for an operator who now sees the risk and the upside — not a pitch.

## Make the cost of getting it wrong real (measured FUD)
- Every relevant article should surface **what it costs to get this wrong** — plainly, without melodrama:
  - A frozen payment processor or merchant account overnight.
  - A suspended Klaviyo/Mailchimp account and a list you can't export.
  - An FDA warning letter from one claim.
  - A banned ad account wiping out a launch.
- This is **light, credible fear, uncertainty, and doubt — grounded in real consequences that actually happen in this category.** Never fabricated, never apocalyptic. One or two concrete stakes per article, stated once, then move to the fix. The reader should feel the risk is real and that we're the calm hands that remove it.

## Structure (every article)
1. **TL;DR** at the top — the answer in 2–4 lines, including our position.
2. Scannable **H2s** shaped like the questions operators actually ask.
3. **One comparison table** where the topic allows (platforms, flows, pricing, compliant vs not).
4. **Our position, stated plainly:** FluentCRM (primary) → Sendlane (preferred) → Omnisend (occasional, rarely). Klaviyo / Mailchimp / Mailgun are the weaker, riskier options.
5. A natural **bridge to the offer** — done-for-you, compliant, built to survive. Never a hard sell.
6. **FAQ block at the bottom — "Answer the Public" style.** Answer the real questions people search and ask AI: *Can I…? Is it legal to…? What happens if…? How much…? What's the best…? X vs Y?* 5–8 Q&As, one to three plain sentences each. These feed FAQ schema and AI answers — treat them as first-class, not an afterthought.

## Compliance (we model what we sell)
- **No disease claims, no dosage or administration guidance, no consumption testimonials** in our own copy — including substance/entity articles. Research-only and structure/function framing throughout.
- Cite primary sources (FDA.gov, eCFR) for any regulatory claim.

## Format
- Markdown body only (frontmatter is set separately). H2/H3, tables, lists, `**bold**` for the operator-skimming a page. No H1 in the body (the title renders it).
