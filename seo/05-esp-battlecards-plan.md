# ESP Battlecards — Plan

A dedicated cluster of **battlecards**: one page per email service provider (ESP), plus head-to-head "X vs Y" pages. Each rides the ESP's brand-name search demand and lands on the same conclusion — most ESPs are the wrong pick for peptides, and the answer is **FluentCRM → Sendlane** (Omnisend rare). This is the highest-leverage way to own the "which platform" decision across every ESP a peptide operator might Google.

Companion to [`03-master-article-list.md`](03-master-article-list.md) and the voice rules in [`04-copywriting-sop.md`](04-copywriting-sop.md).

## Why battlecards

- **Brand-name ESP searches have real, low-competition volume** (klaviyo alternatives 390, omnisend vs klaviyo 260, klaviyo pricing 2.4K, fluentcrm 590, sendlane 480). Every ESP name is a keyword.
- **The buyer is mid-decision.** Someone searching "is Klaviyo good for supplements" or "Brevo vs Mailchimp" is choosing a platform right now. We intercept and redirect to the peptide-safe answer.
- **Topical authority + AI citation.** Covering *every* ESP for one category makes us the definitive source Google and AI assistants cite for "best email platform for peptide/high-risk brands."

## Battlecard template (every ESP page follows this)

Written to [`04-copywriting-sop.md`](04-copywriting-sop.md) — CEO voice, swagger, measured FUD, our record (startup → 7/8 figures), Answer-the-Public FAQ. Structure:

1. **TL;DR verdict** — peptide fit (Best / Workable / Risky / Not a fit) + our one-line recommendation.
2. **What [ESP] is and who it's built for** — honest, no strawman.
3. **Does [ESP] allow peptides / high-risk?** — acceptable-use-policy stance, ban history in-category.
4. **Strengths & weaknesses for a peptide brand specifically** — not a generic review.
5. **Ban / suspension risk** — the measured-FUD beat (frozen account, un-exportable list).
6. **Pricing posture** — rough, honest, no invented numbers.
7. **Our verdict + what we'd run instead** — FluentCRM/Sendlane, with the reasoning.
8. **Comparison row/table** — [ESP] vs FluentCRM vs Sendlane on ban risk / ownership / automation / who it's for.
9. **FAQ** — "Is [ESP] good for supplements?", "Can I use [ESP] for peptides?", "[ESP] vs Klaviyo?", "Will [ESP] ban me?", "[ESP] pricing?", etc.
10. **Internal links** — up to the [best platforms pillar](/blog/best-email-platforms-peptide-supplement-brands), across to 2–3 sibling battlecards, and to the [money hub](/blog/email-marketing-for-peptide-brands).

**Verdict key (assign in the TL;DR):** FluentCRM = Best · Sendlane = Strong · Omnisend = Occasional · everything hosted-and-mainstream (Klaviyo, Mailchimp, ActiveCampaign, Brevo, HubSpot, GetResponse, MailerLite, Constant Contact, Drip, Moosend, Kit, AWeber, Campaign Monitor, Keap, Ontraport) = Risky-to-Not-recommended for pure peptide · transactional APIs (Mailgun, SendGrid, Amazon SES, Postmark) = Not a marketing platform (but SES/Postmark are valid *relays under FluentCRM*).

## Guardrail

Battlecards name competitors and critique their fit for *this category* — keep it factual and defensible (acceptable-use policies, ban patterns), never defamatory. State ban risk as what happens in the category, not as a claim that a named brand "will" ban a specific reader.
